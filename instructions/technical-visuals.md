# Choosing and checking learning visuals

Use this guide to choose, specify and check any authored or generated visual once the learning task is established in [media workflows](media-workflows.md). That workflow owns scope, delivery, style and the compact work record; this guide owns representation, tool capabilities, drawing specification and method-specific checks. The [prompts](media-prompts.md) apply these rules rather than defining another selection policy. Read the decision method and only the relevant tool/verification sections; use the [planning and review prompts](media-prompts.md). The examples are an open repertoire, not a whitelist of diagrams, subjects or tools.

## The quality standard and the choice

Choose jointly from the context, topic, disciplinary conventions, audience and prerequisites, learning operation, nature of the content, necessary precision and intended reading size. A subject name alone never chooses a representation. This template serves secondary, vocational, higher and other education: correctness and professional visual quality do not decrease with the learner's level. Adjust explanatory support, abstraction and detail, not truth. Do not infer prerequisites from age or school type alone.

Aim for the greatest precision warranted by the claim and evidence: correct geometry where it matters, notation, relationships, data, conditions and deliberate composition. This is not a demand for maximal detail, photorealism, unnecessary decimals or unsupported certainty. A clear schematic can be professionally exact; a highly detailed model can obscure the lesson.

Artistic illustrations, infographics and banners are full teaching options in any subject, including mathematics, natural sciences and professional studies. They can be the best primary representation for recognition, context, conceptual understanding, an application or an intelligible analogy. Their beauty can invite useful attention. They are not merely decoration or a last resort. Conversely, aesthetic detail does not establish a scientific, anatomical, historical or other factual claim.

There is no universal ranking of tools. A technical drawing is a poor substitute for an accessible conceptual overview when it does not serve that task; an attractive overview is a poor substitute for a required quantitative or formal explanation. Several complementary representations may be right. Redraw required notebook/board diagrams as the same drawing in the same role, correct, under [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md), regardless of additional imagery. Only meaning-bearing layout is binding; improve clutter and handwriting unevenness. Professional correctness comes first, with a lasting correction label and private evidence of changes; never a notebook-reconstruction caption. Apply the same necessary fidelity to newly authored explanations. Correct source errors through the existing labeled-correction workflow, not silent reproduction or alteration.

## Standardized symbols and prescribed signs

When recognizing or interpreting a prescribed sign is part of the learning task, its exact identity is teaching content. This applies across subjects and media: chemical hazard pictograms, fire-protection and evacuation signs, mandatory/prohibition/warning signs, transport labels, traffic signs, electrical symbols and other regulated or standardized notation. These examples are not a closed list.

1. **Identify before drawing.** Establish the applicable system, intended use, jurisdiction and relevant version from an authoritative source; record the sign identifier when available. Do not assume every familiar sign is internationally standardized. Do not interchange chemical-container GHS/CLP pictograms, dangerous-goods transport labels and workplace safety signs. A historical sign may be taught as historical; do not silently modernize a notebook transcription.
2. **Reuse verified artwork.** Prefer the responsible standards body or authority; an authoritative institution's verified distribution is also suitable. Confirm lawful reuse and keep the source URL, retrieval date, sign identifier and original file hash in the evidence record. Store reusable teaching assets in their normal repository location with attribution; the full standards publication is not automatically redistributable. A downloadable file or third-party icon library alone does not prove compliance.
3. **Preserve the sign.** Keep its required geometry, symbol details, colors, border, orientation and clear space. Resize proportionally only; do not crop, stretch, mirror, recolor for the theme, simplify or decorate it. A prescribed directional variant must be selected and verified, not created by an arbitrary flip. A faithful export must be checked against the original. Add translations, explanations, arrows and captions outside the sign without obscuring it. In dark mode preserve the prescribed background; a transparent source must not turn a white field black.
4. **Keep generative art outside the sign.** Do not prompt an image model to draw or repair a real sign. If a banner or infographic needs one, reserve space and insert the verified asset deterministically, then inspect the complete result. Omit an incidental sign when it has no teaching purpose rather than include a plausible fake. Exact conventional notation may be produced by a validated CAD/symbol library where appropriate; verify the applicable convention and geometry, not merely the software's name.
5. **Check meaning as well as appearance.** Explain the relevant meaning and limitations precisely; one icon can cover several hazard classes and does not replace a complete label or instructions. Do not infer a one-to-one old/new mapping from visual similarity. Compare source and final image, identifier, caption and context; inspect embedded phone/print views, including dark backgrounds and any export. Grayscale must not be described as color-faithful where color is part of the standard. Rendering successfully is not a compliance check.
6. **No counterfeit fallback.** If the applicable symbol or reusable asset cannot be verified, use a textual explanation and authoritative link, record the concrete gap, and seek clarification only if it changes what can be taught. Do not supply a hand-drawn or generated look-alike, even labeled "not official", as the recognition example. A source's inaccurate sketch may be described as source evidence, but the teaching page must separate it from the verified sign and any labeled correction.

Reviewers flag the exact affected sign and discrepancy (wrong system/version, artwork, color, proportions, direction, caption, reuse provenance or missing context). Correct only with supported evidence. An educational depiction is not certification that a real workplace, product label or installation complies with a standard.

## From the problem to a drawing

1. **Establish the task and context.** Reuse the lesson's valid scope decision and sources; consult relevant curriculum passages only for unresolved relevance/depth questions. Name what the learner should recognize, compare, explain, follow or calculate, and the prerequisites needed. Identify the required source diagrams that must remain available.
2. **Identify the meaning to make visible.** Which facts, values, conditions, relations, cases or spatial/temporal features carry the answer? What is known, approximate, hypothetical or disputed? Distinguish measured distance from layout spacing, chronology from causality, depiction from evidence and a prescribed motion from a physical simulation.
3. **Choose a representation for that operation.** Consider a suitable existing visual, a table or prose as well as a new image, judging all by how well they support the learning operation; none wins by default. For a consequential ambiguity, briefly compare plausible alternatives by what becomes understandable and what may be misread. Do not impose a scoring matrix on every picture. Prefer manageable complexity without sacrificing informative detail or artistic teaching value. Use 3D, several views or animation when they reveal a useful relation. The no-new-teaching-image option does not remove the existing page-header requirement.
4. **Specify the actual drawing.** In the existing visual plan, describe the applicable items below. A diagram name or a request for a beautiful picture is not a drawing specification.
5. **Choose available capabilities and a faithful fallback.** Use the toolkit below, including combinations or unlisted tools/types when justified. Confirm the actual rendering/export path. If availability or final reading size makes the plan unsuitable, revise the representation while preserving its teaching task. A missing convenience renderer is not a diagram ban.
6. **Check meaning and presentation separately.** Define the relevant failure conditions before production, verify the source/model/construction, then inspect the actual exported image and its page context. A technically valid image may still be the wrong explanation. Use the applicable checks below, not every check for every image.
7. **Finish and retain the evidence.** Keep the editable basis, source/data versions, relevant production settings and final checks in the existing record. Stop when the authorized task is met; do not regenerate an accepted suitable image to showcase a different tool.

### A drawing specification that can be executed

Use the existing compact visual plan: this is not another record type or mandatory form with empty fields. A simple figure may need only a few lines, while a complex technical or multi-panel explanation needs more.

* **Content:** exact elements, data/functions or relationships; source locations; conditions and distinct cases; uncertainty and declared simplifications.
* **Meaning mapped to marks:** what each axis, position, length, area, group, symbol, color, edge, arrow or movement means, and what is only layout. Distinguish different kinds of relation. Do not leave substantive decisions to the renderer or image model.
* **Composition:** focus, viewpoint, reading order, projection/cutaway, panel relationships, relevant common scale and how the learner finds the important feature. Choose abstraction for the lesson rather than accepting a tool's defaults.
* **Visible text:** final necessary labels, context, symbols, units and explanations. Preserve the taught notation. Specify callout endpoints where identification matters.
* **Construction:** input parameters/data, segmentation or structure building, geometric/layout operations, annotations, rendering/export and any assembly of layers. The specification must enable source code, an authored diagram or a precise provider prompt to be produced.
* **Acceptance:** which observable relation establishes correctness, which learner question the image should answer, and which plausible misreading must be prevented. Record consequential limits where the learner needs them; keep tool metadata out of the lesson.

For example, plotting `1/x` requires separate branches, the excluded zero, the visible plotting window, distinguished asymptotes if shown, labels and independent sample-value checks. The window is not the mathematical domain. For torque, a recognizable application scene, a force/moment-arm construction and an angle–torque plot answer different questions; they need different specifications, with compatible notation if presented together.

### What the form itself claims

Prepare the reader to interpret the visual before relying on it: introduce unfamiliar symbols, conventions and the question it answers. Follow [context and omissions](note-formatting.md#context-before-the-learner-needs-it) when a source lacks a detail. Keep editorial statements such as "the notebook does not label the moment-arm length" in adjacent prose with the supported interpretation or limit; they are not diagram labels. Do not remove meaningful unknowns, exact source geometry or material uncertainty, and do not silently turn a derived completion into a source-given value.

Before choosing a form, identify its consequential implicit claims: complete ordering, simultaneity, continuity, uniform intervals, exclusive categories, proportional length/area, causal direction, connectivity or exact position. Check whether the evidence supports them. If not, change the encoding or mark uncertainty visibly (for example a range, approximate label or explained uncertain edge); do not rely on a caveat hidden in evidence. Also ask what the form **cannot** convey that the task requires: recognition, context, purpose, location, proportion or sequence. A picture with individually correct details can still fail because it does not communicate that needed meaning. Apply this principle to unlisted forms as well as familiar ones.

## Capabilities, combinations and fallbacks

These are possible capabilities, not claims that the programs are installed or that all their outputs have been tested. The toolkit is extensible. Consult current primary documentation when a new capability or notation is uncertain.

| Tool or method | What can be built from it | Boundary or useful alternative |
|---|---|---|
| **Matplotlib** | Data coordinates, axes/scales, points, lines, shapes, fills, annotations and panels; custom diagrams as well as named chart functions | Explicit data/functions and semantic mapping are required. Supplement with authored SVG/TikZ for a difficult exact symbol or layout. Automatic axes and smooth lines do not validate the model. |
| **Graphviz** | Nodes, edges, labels, groups and layout constraints for trees, networks and other relationships | Layout is not measured geometry, elapsed time or linguistic analysis. Control meaningful order or use authored coordinates/notation when necessary. |
| **Mermaid** | Text-authored flow, sequence, state and other supported diagram families | Check the target viewer/version and export. A suitable PlantUML, Graphviz or authored SVG route can replace an unsupported feature without changing the meaning. |
| **PlantUML** | Diagram languages with elements for actions, states, participants, messages, timing and structural relations, among others | Do not force a concept into a misleading UML construct because convenient syntax exists. Another notation or authored SVG/TikZ may fit better. |
| **Authored SVG / TikZ** | Geometric primitives, paths, text, symbols and controlled placement for custom 2D constructions and formal notation | SVG is a format; TikZ is a typesetting/drawing system with its own runtime. Both require a sound construction and readable export. General drawing freedom can carry more maintenance work. |
| **FreeCAD** | Parametric geometry, constraints, solid operations, sections and projections using the actual available workbench/API | Useful when shape, dimensions or linked views matter. A small 2D construction or conceptual picture may serve another task better. Valid solids do not establish engineering design or standards compliance. |
| **POV-Ray** | Spatial primitives and constructed scenes, cameras, materials/light and parameterized poses | Useful for spatial recognition or an explanatory scene. Physical motion requires a separate justified model; perspective is unsuitable for direct length comparison unless its effects are handled. |
| **Source-based mapping** | A checked geographic base or coordinate data, using a suitable mapping/projection method, plotted layers or authored SVG | Use justified positions, geographic projection and source/date. A clearly labeled schematic can explain topology without claiming geographic accuracy; generated scenery is not a geographic base. |
| **Generative image model** | Artistic illustration, recognizable context, visual analogy, atmosphere and conceptual overview with author-selected facts and composition | A full teaching route in any subject. It cannot guarantee exact geometry/data/notation. Preserve exact layers through authored construction when needed, following the existing image workflow. |

**Computation is a separate choice.** NumPy supplies numerical arrays/operations and is a required Matplotlib dependency. SciPy is optional for appropriate computations such as fitting, optimization or differential equations. Spyder is an IDE, not a prerequisite for automated rendering. A solver's output and the plotted result need separate checks. See [Matplotlib dependencies](https://matplotlib.org/stable/install/dependencies.html), [NumPy](https://numpy.org/doc/stable/user/whatisnumpy.html) and [SciPy](https://scipy.org/).

**Fallbacks preserve the task, not just a file extension.** An unfamiliar chart can be constructed from Matplotlib artists or SVG primitives; a missing formal renderer can be replaced with carefully authored notation; several linked 2D views may substitute for 3D if the same spatial reasoning remains possible. A static image is not equivalent when interactive rotation or timing is essential. A suitable existing image or authored illustration may replace unavailable generation if it still communicates the intended recognition/context. In either direction, preserve necessary meaning, precision, readability and practical maintainability. Do not silently deliver a different learning task. If no suitable route is available, report the specific gap and keep an essential unfinished visual marked as such; supported text need not be blocked by an optional image.

New diagram names do not require additions to an allowlist or a new permission request within the authorized task. New installations, providers or spending are separate from diagram choice; use the existing setup and authorization workflow. Private source must not be sent to a public renderer by default.

## Open examples: choose the meaning, then the form

These examples illustrate reasoning, not an exhaustive classification or mandatory routing table. Unlisted/custom/mixed forms are welcome when they fit better.

| What should become understandable? | Possible representation | What must not be confused? |
|---|---|---|
| Recognition, parts, or where something is | Annotated object/scene, cutaway, detail view, source-based map or plan | Correct identity, callout target and spatial meaning; a schematic is not a measured map |
| Comparison or classification | Matched views, panels, groups, sets or hierarchy | Common criteria/scale where needed; preserve overlaps and do not invent a false binary |
| Why a mechanism works | An explanatory scene, worked example or justified causal structure | Association or chronology alone is not evidence of a cause |
| A numerical relationship, change or value | Function, scatter, line, step or other appropriate quantitative plot, often Matplotlib | Data coordinates versus layout; continuous functions versus discrete observations |
| Categories, distribution or proportions | Dot/bar chart, histogram, box plot, heatmap or a table | Counts versus density; a proportion's denominator; an arbitrary category order versus progression |
| Several numerical characteristics | A radar chart if meaningful; otherwise dot/bar panels or a table | Different scales, units and criterion directions; polygon area is not an automatic overall score |
| Relationships, dependencies, hierarchy | Graph, tree, concept map, radial map | Association, descent, causality and flow are different edges; a network need not be a tree |
| Actions and choices | Activity/flow diagram, optionally with responsibility lanes | Action versus persistent state; alternative branches versus concurrent activities |
| A system's states and transitions | State diagram | State, event, guard and action; not merely a list of tasks |
| Exchanges between participants in order | Sequence diagram | Message order versus duration or physical movement |
| When states persist or overlap | Timing diagram or parallel time bands | Exact versus approximate time, order versus duration, scale versus spacing |
| Chronology or a justified schedule | Timeline or parallel bands; Gantt where actual scheduling semantics fit | Temporal sequence does not prove cause; historical uncertainty is not an exact timestamp |
| A formal/linguistic/geometric construction | Authored SVG/TikZ, typeset structure or another exact construction | The taught convention versus a convenient different notation |
| Shape, dimensions, section and projection | CAD model/views or an adequate authored construction | Geometric validity versus design/standard validity; perspective versus measurable projection |
| Spatial recognition or change | 3D scene, linked views or justified animation | Depiction, kinematics and physical simulation |
| Context, application, conceptual overview or analogy | Artistic illustration, infographic or a separate banner | Illustrative/metaphorical detail versus a factual claim or a precise model |

The same topic can lead to several rows. A battle may need terrain and phase maps, parallel event bands or a sequence of orders, depending on the question and evidence. A royal family tree explains kinship; a succession rule or competing claim needs separately identified legal/political relationships; reign bands explain overlap. Do not invent precise timing, a universal inheritance rule or causality to fit the chart. These are transferable examples, not new subject requirements.

### Meaning-specific constraints

Use form-specific constraints only where relevant:

* Sequence: identify what the order means, actual stages, transitions and whether spacing is time-scaled. Chronology does not prove causation, improvement or a cycle.
* Contrast: keep the same criteria and the viewpoint, scale, units and notation needed for an interpretable comparison; distinguish typical cases, theoretical endpoints and extremes. Do not turn a continuum into a false binary or imply a numerical scale without data.
* Relationships: choose topology from semantic links, with endpoints, direction, labels and conditions. A hub exchanging with its neighbors is not a circular process. Check EVERY visible arrow, including unexpected ones.
* Grouping: identify membership or part-whole basis; preserve overlaps. Proximity does not imply causation or rank.
* Metaphor: name the intended correspondence and important misleading implications to avoid. Do not let decoration masquerade as a literal fact.
* OTHER: recognize the actual content and choose a justified novel or mixed representation, reuse, or no new image. Infographics need learning value; banners can be decoration.


### Historical time, space and changing relationships

Use source-supported dates and positions. Mark approximate/disputed dates and uncertainty intervals visibly; distinguish an elapsed-time scale from an order-only layout. Do not force an unknown relative order into a definite total sequence. Model a partial order or mark the uncertainty. A message's delivery delay requires its own evidence/label; arrow spacing alone does not establish it.

Specify the calendar/year convention when consequential. Conventional BCE/CE chronology has no year zero; astronomical year numbering differs. Convert explicitly for numerical plotting across eras, and do not silently combine incompatible calendars or precision levels. These checks are triggered by the content, not mandatory metadata on every timeline.

A battle's successive-position maps need a suitable source-based geographic base with permitted reuse, or a visibly schematic layout that makes no geographic-precision claim. Check terrain/locations, directions, phase dates and uncertainty as relevant. Changing alliances, kinship, orders or competing succession claims need distinct edge meanings and time applicability where consequential; several dated views can be clearer than one ambiguous network. A family tree does not by itself establish succession.

When reusing a formal renderer for a historical or other unfamiliar domain, omit irrelevant default notation or explain the marks actually used. Activation bars, return arrows and lifeline endings must not acquire invented domain meaning. A technical-looking diagram is not stronger historical evidence.

### Formal diagrams and language

For PlantUML or another formal notation, keep action/control (activity), state/event/guard (state), participant/message order (sequence) and duration/overlap (timing) distinct. Check branch conditions and completeness within the declared scope, joins/concurrency, transition triggers, message direction/participants and actual time units as applicable. Explain unfamiliar necessary symbols, and avoid formalism that does not help the learner. A render success is not semantic validation. See the official [activity](https://plantuml.com/activity-diagram-beta), [state](https://plantuml.com/state-diagram), [sequence](https://plantuml.com/sequence-diagram) and [timing](https://plantuml.com/timing-diagram) references.

Other forms such as class/object, component/deployment, use case, WBS or unfamiliar domain notation are possible. Verify their particular semantics rather than treating their arrows as generic links. For example, inheritance is not any association, multiplicity is not an invented number, and a work breakdown is not chronological order.

For grammatical/formal analysis, preserve words, morpheme boundaries, order, labels, cases and the taught analysis system. A dependency tree, constituency tree and classroom underlining are not interchangeable. Graphviz arranges the supplied structure; it does not establish the analysis. Retain meaningful order even if a different layout looks neater, and never remove a real cross-link just to obtain a tree. Use authored/type-set notation if a graph tool cannot express the taught form clearly.

### Spider diagrams

Resolve from context whether "spider" means a radial conceptual map or a numerical radar chart; ask only if the unresolved distinction materially changes the result. Branch length in a concept map is ordinarily layout, not quantity. A radar needs defined axes, ranges, units/normalization and consistent scales across compared cases. Explain any relevant criterion-direction or normalization difference; preserve access to the raw values. Changing category order changes polygon appearance, so area is not an automatic total or ranking. Consider dot/bar panels or a table when they make differences clearer. [Matplotlib radar example](https://matplotlib.org/stable/gallery/specialty_plots/radar_chart.html).

## Quantitative and numerical verification

Apply the checks relevant to the claim, including custom diagrams made from general primitives. Record the actual calculated value, comparison or observation in the existing evidence record, not a checklist number or an unsupported “passed.”

1. **Evidence and data meaning:** identify the actual function/data and source version. Separate measurement, historical estimate, computed model and synthetic teaching example. Mark examples as examples. Missing data is not zero; interpolation is not measurement; a plot does not establish causality or validity beyond its assumptions.
2. **Variables and conventions:** verify units/conversion, signs, axes, domain, coordinate orientation and time/category ordering. Preserve meaningful disciplinary conventions rather than adopting defaults: for example, motion along an economic curve and a shift of that curve are different claims.
3. **Domain and discontinuities:** show excluded points, open/closed ends, piecewise conditions and asymptotes correctly. Never connect `1/x` across zero. Simply deleting a bad point can still connect its neighbors; use separate segments or appropriate masking/NaN. See [Matplotlib gaps](https://matplotlib.org/stable/gallery/lines_bars_and_markers/masked_demo.html).
4. **Sampling and construction:** check the relevant critical points, roots, extrema, fast changes and independent known values. More samples cannot repair a wrong formula/domain. Distinguish exact from approximated values; verify the actual constructed coordinates, not only the parameters supplied to the code.
5. **Scales and encoded quantities:** justify linear/log scales, cropping, broken axes, common panel limits and geometric aspect ratio. Length-encoded bars normally need a zero baseline; a function plot does not always do so. Never silently omit invalid log-scale values. Equal axis scaling matters when geometric shape/angle is read, but is not a blanket requirement for differently dimensioned x/y variables.
6. **Family-specific interpretation:** check histogram bins/counts/density, percentage denominators, set-region membership, conditional probability branches or other relevant mathematics. Do not imply intermediate values for arbitrary categories or discrete observations. Area only represents quantity when constructed accordingly.
7. **Uncertainty and fitting:** use only justified error bars/bands and identify their meaning. Distinguish observed points from a fitted/interpolated curve; smoothing is not new knowledge. Unknown uncertainty is not a license to invent a band.
8. **Solver/model checks:** for a numerical calculation, verify assumptions, initial/boundary conditions, units, solver status and relevant residual, invariant or known solution. Where sensitive, check tolerance/resolution dependence. A successful solve or smooth animation cannot validate the physical model; choose checks using the actual solver's documentation.
9. **Final export:** check labels, accents, math, legends, rounding, arrowheads, clipping and legibility. Confirm that the encoded values and claimed conclusion agree with the prose. Large pixel dimensions alone do not establish readability on a phone or A4 page.

## Verify the meaningful detail, regardless of tool

1. Identify what the learner is supposed to infer and which visible detail carries that claim. Decide which parts must be exact and which may be simplified. An icon can be schematic, but cannot contradict the function it is used to explain. Simplification must not remove the lesson's subject.
2. Establish the source, parameters, units, relevant drawing convention and applicability. Do not invent an unspecified standard or dimensional tolerance. A model library or a renderer is not the authority for the lesson.
3. Check the actual constructed result against the claim using the appropriate independent method: calculations, geometric intersections, graph structure, source comparison or the applicable notation rules. Merely reading back supplied parameters is insufficient. Choose a check whose failure would expose a meaningful error.
4. Inspect the final rendered/exported view too: projection, cropping, line styles, labels, attachment points, scale and readability. A valid model can have a misleading picture, and a beautiful picture can have an invalid model. Inspect composite graphics after assembly.
5. Record source/model versions, meaningful checks, final artifact hashes and remaining limitations in the existing evidence record. Distinguish a sampled check from a full validation. Never claim manufacturing, structural or standards certification from an educational illustration.

Examples: if a gear's profile or engagement is taught, check the actual tooth geometry and relevant parameters, including the generator's limitations; a gear-shaped star is insufficient. For a thread, identify the intended profile and axial spacing, handedness and number of starts where relevant; a decorative spiral is not a specification. For a wall or section, verify the source's line/hatch convention and what it denotes; a software pattern name does not establish its meaning. The same principle applies to axes, circuit symbols, chemical bonds, grammatical relationships and other meaningful details.

## Animation and runtime

Animate only when change, sequence or spatial behavior adds understanding. Define whether it is a prescribed sequence of poses, a kinematic model or a physical simulation. State a consequential simplification for the learner; keep software metadata in evidence. A pauseable animation should have a useful static counterpart for printing and inaccessible playback.

Check relevant constraints across the generated sequence numerically where possible. Inspect initial/final states, extrema, contacts and other critical transitions, then the final playback. Sparse frame sampling alone does not validate every intermediate state. Record exactly which frames or invariants were checked and distinguish technical playback testing from a person actually watching it.

Measure with a small representative case before committing to a large run. Record tool/version, hardware or VM resources, resolution, quality, thread count, number of frames and wall time; separate model construction, rendering and encoding where available. Repeat short cases and report their spread. Do not extrapolate a trivial scene into a guaranteed cost for a complex one. A VM comparison uses the same source revision and settings, then separately tests any proposed resource change.

Make a POV-Ray animation (the scene's `clock` runs 0 to 1) with `python3 tools/visual_tools.py render povray <scene.pov> --frames 24 --fps 12 --output wiki/assets/<dir>`; it writes `figure.mp4` and its middle frame `figure.png`.
Embed only the video, `![alt](../assets/<dir>/figure.mp4)`: the site plays it and print/PDF shows the `figure.png` poster.

The [optional pilot](../examples/technical-visuals/README.md) provides checked example sources, measured limits and previews. It is not an automatic installation or a requirement to adopt those tools.

## Delivery and reproducibility

Inspect the final embedded view at its intended phone and print size, not only a zoomed preview. Where A4 printing is intended, verify the actual placement and grayscale readability. Important differences need labels, line styles or shapes as well as color. Simplify focus or split naturally when crowded; do not shrink essential text until it is unreadable. Preserve the configured language, accents and disciplinary notation.

If a target reading channel cannot render the diagram language (for example Mermaid or PlantUML), supply and inspect a suitable static export; do not assume a successful view in another client covers it. Keep useful alt text and the main learning facts in visible prose; for data plots make the relevant values accessible in text/table form where appropriate. An internal image-description comment is not an accessible substitute. An animation needs an informative static counterpart; check the final PDF/viewer as well as the original SVG. A picture that needs zoom should say so honestly, but zoom must not be the only way to obtain the essential explanation.

Keep the editable SVG, diagram text, plotting/model script or appropriate native source and the lawful data/parameters or exact private source reference. Preserve existing privacy/licensing/storage boundaries. Small shareable source and necessary exports belong in their repository locations; binaries, secrets and large intermediate renders do not. In the existing evidence record, include the relevant production command/settings, tool version, dependencies/fonts when consequential, source/data versions, actual checks and final artifact hash. Record a random seed when used; neither a seed nor an image prompt promises byte-identical regeneration across environments. For generated images, retain the accepted actual artifact and its existing job/evidence record.

Distinguish **documented capability**, **available execution path**, **rendered example**, and **checked result**. Evidence supports only the actual version, input, representation, export path and checks performed. Do not create a registry of every possible diagram or treat the absence of a previous sample as a ban. Validate the relevant path on first use and reuse applicable evidence without claiming it covers a new substantive case. The optional pilot demonstrates particular Graphviz/FreeCAD/POV-Ray examples; it does not establish universal support or deployment of Matplotlib, PlantUML or any other tool.

## Human feedback

When the user asks to review samples, keep a discoverable pending list in the existing planning/review record: artifact/link and version or hash, machine checks separately, human status and the specific response when it arrives. Until the user explicitly says they viewed it, record it as not yet viewed; silence, time passing or a general acknowledgment is not visual acceptance. Viewing and accepting are separate states. This tracks a requested review; it does not add a universal approval gate to authorized note work.
