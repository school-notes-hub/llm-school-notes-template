# Visual policy

Shared operating rules, loaded through [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md). Named rules in other modules are located through its rule map.

## Choosing and replacing authored visuals

* **Choose by learning function.** Keep editable SVG/code diagrams for exact geometry, vectors, force directions, dimensions, mathematical/formal structures, linguistic analyses, graph/set relationships, formulas and other precision-dependent content. Keep simple Mermaid/SVG where it is clear; existing accepted banners are not regenerated en masse. Banners and infographics always use an image plan and `sn gen`, with independent figure review. A new topic, summary and subject index gets a banner; lesson logs reuse it with `banner_from`; a text edit on an existing page never requires one. Include an infographic where an overview teaches more than prose, at most one per topic page. Notebook, teacher and precise teaching drawings use editable drawn representations. This is not a subject-based or blanket SVG-to-PNG conversion: historical maps and flow arrows also carry exact claims. The current selected infographic model is `openai/gpt-image-2.5-sunburst` through OpenRouter; its availability, authorization and spending limit must be established in the actual environment before paid calls. Missing access does not block the supported lesson or justify replacing a good existing image with a worse placeholder.
* **Preserve precision-dependent teaching diagrams.** Treat every precision-dependent teaching diagram in the notebook or teacher material to learn as lesson content, including mathematical/formal structures, linguistic analyses, quantitative graphs and technical drawings. Redraw it as the same drawing in the same role, correct; only the meaning-bearing layout is binding; the figure never refers to the notebook. Use a precise, editable representation such as SVG, code or typeset notation. Preserve the taught conventions and distinct cases, correcting unambiguous errors with the lasting correction label. Meaning-bearing layout includes the side on which a force acts, above/below, connections and step order; improve crowding, slanted layout, overlapping labels and handwriting unevenness. The learner should recognize their drawing at first glance; professional correctness always takes precedence. Every notebook drawing is required, including copies of board or textbook drawings and photographed hand-drawn teacher diagrams; prose cannot replace it. A banner or generated infographic does not replace it. Compare the source diagrams with the resulting page through the existing two-way self-check coverage record; do not create a parallel ledger. Check an unclear or apparently incorrect feature against the source and retain a labeled correction or open question; do not invent it or silently omit it. No subject is excluded from useful infographics: a page may combine precise diagrams with a generated infographic when they serve distinct useful learning tasks. Preservation requires retaining the independently editable, deterministically rendered diagram and making it available on the page. A generator’s redrawing never counts as preservation, even when supplied with an exact reference. For a composite, assemble the precise layer from that authored source without generative redrawing, compare it after assembly and retain a link to the separate precise diagram.
* **Adapt visual style to meaning.** Choose the banner/infographic's visual language from the supported topic and relevant context, such as period, place, application or the world of a literary work, rather than imposing one look by subject name. Reuse coherent styling within a related topic or series when helpful. Preserve legibility, consistent semantic colors and notation, and meaningful distinctions without relying on color alone. Materials, clothing, objects and emotional tone must not introduce unsupported reconstruction, anachronism or distorted interpretation. Do not present a generated scene as an authentic source photograph, document or artwork. Prefer simpler illustrative detail where evidence is insufficient. The concrete choices belong in the existing image plan and provider prompt under [media workflows](../instructions/media-workflows.md#banner-and-infographic-design-decisions), not a new mandatory subject taxonomy.
* **Verify meaningful visual details with the appropriate method.** Across SVG, CAD, generated pictures and animation, identify which geometry, notation, relationship or motion carries the teaching claim. Check the actual constructed result against its source and applicable parameters/conventions, then inspect the final rendered view. A successful render or plausible appearance does not verify a gear profile, thread spacing, wall symbol, graph or other precise claim. Use the [visual selection guide](../instructions/technical-visuals.md) for context-sensitive representation/tool choice, a concrete drawing specification, faithful fallbacks, relevant checks and animation limits; preserve the existing source coverage and evidence record.
* **Choose examples with a visible reason to care.** An explanatory illustration should use a recognizable, realistic problem or contrast that makes the taught relationship matter. Physical correctness alone is insufficient: compare plausible alternatives and choose the one that makes the benefit or distinction easiest to understand without contrived behavior or assumed specialist experience. The finished image must support that reading; do not infer a lesson that is only present in the prompt. Keep a banner uncluttered and leave calculations and precise constructions to the page.
* **Choose form from the learning task.** Select a visual from what the learner needs to recognize, explain, compare or do, the nature of the content and the actual reading size. Not every infographic is a relationship diagram; plan topology only when relationships matter to the learning task. The [visual selection guide](../instructions/technical-visuals.md) provides an open, capability-based repertoire: if none fits, justify a custom or mixed representation, reuse a suitable visual or choose no new image. Subject name, orientation or a previously successful example alone does not determine composition.
* **Replace selectively and verify.** For a requested improvement, inspect the actual current rendering, all affected page uses and the supporting evidence. Record keep, improve-svg or replace-with-png with a reason in the relevant evidence record. Generate a replacement into a new file while keeping the old image and links. Only after direct content, label, arrow/condition and phone-width checks pass, update all affected image links, alt text, captions, prose, descriptions and evidence-index entries together. A prettier image with lost conditions is not an accepted replacement. For print media also apply the specified A4 check. No mass replacement or paid regeneration is implied by adding this rule.
* **Describe the actual image for later agents.** The independent figure checker's review holds the observed description (key labels, relationships, arrow directions, conditions, limitations), written after directly checking the final image - the generation prompt describes intention and must not be substituted for observation. `sn close` writes the `image-description` comment, the hash and the evidence record from it.
* **Preserve evidence and appropriate storage.** Teacher images with unclear rights stay private evidence; the licensed insertion route is defined in *Images from material to learn*. Otherwise use independent visuals or independently acquired reusable originals under *Images from material to learn* in [Sources and evidence](sources-and-evidence.md). Acquired evidence remains unchanged; a generated lookalike cannot replace a real object used for recognition. Small authored lesson images may stay in `wiki/assets/` with relative links for offline and review access. Large standalone media follows the configured private external-media policy; do not assume a Drive share or image-hosting link is already available. Keep previous authored versions when needed to reconstruct evidence. Do not copy or trace prohibited textbook images through a generator.
* **Generate during ingest as well as on request.** The writer assesses the current lesson's visual needs and runs the image workflow in its own pass (*In a local pass* in [media workflows](media-workflows.md)): plan, generate, inspect and repair within limits, then leave the marker and the commission and hand the figure over to the independent figure checker; `sn close` inserts only after independent acceptance. No per-image confirmation is needed within the configured budget, which `sn gen` enforces.
* **Decide before drawing.** For relevant lesson sections, choose no new image, reuse an appropriate checked asset, precise SVG/code, or a generated infographic according to the learning goal and actual reading size. Explain meaningful choices briefly in the existing ingest/evidence record, not a separate catalogue. Do not add an infographic to every section. In statics, for example, a conceptual overview may use a generated image while a force construction retains precise editable geometry; a subject name alone never selects or prohibits a format. Existing-image replacement still requires the full replacement checks above.
  * *Part of the writer's pass, not a separate round.* The writer makes this decision for the sections the new material affects and produces the figures it calls for in the same pass as the text. There is no separate visual round and no separate visual review: the one independent review of the change checks the text and the figures together and, as the independent figure checker, gives the figure verdict that insertion needs. The writer repairs a rejected figure once, and the same reviewer confirms only that figure; this is not a new review round.
  * *Inserted figures in edited sections.* When the pass changes the text of a section that holds an already inserted figure, that figure's verdict no longer holds. The pass's one review actually looks at that figure against the new text and gives its verdict; only its `accept` renews the verdict, never a refresh without a look. Look only where it is needed: only the figures whose verdict the edit invalidated, and only at what the edit could change that the figure must agree with; no other figure is reopened. An accept holds only for the content the reviewer saw: any later change of the section - a sentence the reviewer proposed included, even word for word - sends the figure to the confirmation pass. Nothing is regenerated for this: if the figure's content is still correct, only its verdict is renewed; if a clarification of the text, still correct on its own, resolves a mismatch, that is the fix; a replacement (`replaces`) is made only when the figure's content is outdated or no longer correct (`decision_reason` `a` or `b`; a reviewer's request alone, `c`, does not justify one here). If the review rejects it, the writer fixes it once in one of these two ways, and the same reviewer confirms only that figure; this is not a new review round.
  * *On request, named pages only.* The owner may also ask for this decision, its figures and their review for named pages. Then decide and draw only for those pages, in the same way (one writer pass, one review); the request never extends to other pages.
* **Respect one scope and budget.** Preserve the current lesson's core, prerequisites, selected depth and exact assertions. At most three attempts per image (the first generation and two repairs), counted by `sn gen` across passes. Reuse an already accepted asset when the source version and intended use still match. At the limit, only an independently accepted usable candidate may be inserted; otherwise the figure is not inserted, its marker is removed and the reason recorded; cosmetic imperfections may remain with a recorded limitation.

* **Other figures and replacements.** For every other figure, decide whether it improves learning, understanding or deeper understanding beyond prose; if so, include it, choosing the form and tool without a closed type list. State what the reader must understand or do and what misreading to avoid; the text must also teach without the figure. A replacement requires `decision_reason` with `a` (the image claims what the text no longer does), `b` (its scope is outdated) or `c` (a reader/figure reviewer requests it), plus the concrete reason. Taste alone is insufficient. For precise teaching drawings, prefer editing the free SVG/Mermaid route first. Use a new ID and `replaces`, keep the old image until acceptance, and allow at most one replacement per image per pass. `sn close` inserts only after the independent figure check; no self-acceptance in any mode.
